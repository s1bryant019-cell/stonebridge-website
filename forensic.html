Areas of service

Forensic services considered by referral

Dr. Stephen W. Bryant, PsyD, offers a distinct professional pathway for legal-system questions. The nature of the referral determines whether an independent evaluation, records review, or other consultation is appropriate; not every referral or evaluation type can be accepted.

Forensic psychological evaluations

Independent psychological assessment addressing a defined legal or psycholegal referral question, when the requested examination is within the clinician's competence and available resources. Procedures and reporting are specified in a written agreement.

Records review and professional consultation

Case-specific review of relevant documentation and consultation for attorneys, courts, or other authorized referral sources. The scope, intended audience, and limits of any resulting opinions are clarified in advance.

Legal-related treatment consultation

When appropriately authorized, a treating clinician may discuss clinical treatment with a guardian ad litem, attorney, or other legal professional. This limited communication is not an independent forensic evaluation or custody recommendation.

How referrals work

A defined referral process protects the integrity of the work.

01 · Referral

Describe the legal question

Contact the practice with your professional role, the general referral question, the requested work, and any relevant deadlines. Do not send sensitive records until secure transfer arrangements have been confirmed.

02 · Scope review

Confirm role and fit

The practice reviews possible conflicts, jurisdictional or court requirements, assessment methods, available resources, and whether an independent forensic role is appropriate.

03 · Written agreement

Establish terms before work

Accepted matters receive a separate agreement detailing scope, fees, responsible payer, payment arrangements, informed consent or notification, records, and anticipated deliverables.

Professional fees

Fees are established before services begin.

Legal-related professional services generally fall outside psychotherapy insurance benefits. Independent forensic evaluations, extensive records review, reports, depositions, and court testimony are quoted based on the referral, estimated work, and any applicable court order.

Written arrangements identify who is responsible for payment. A release of information alone does not establish financial responsibility, and legally required disclosures are handled according to applicable law.

Professional boundaries

The treating and forensic roles remain distinct.

A psychologist treating a patient cannot automatically serve as that patient's independent forensic evaluator. Forensic referrals are screened for conflicts and limitations. Communications about an existing patient require the applicable authorization or legal basis and remain limited to the appropriate clinical role.

The practice does not guarantee a particular forensic conclusion or outcome. Availability, service format, testing requirements, and the suitability of remote methods are determined on a case-by-case basis. For outpatient psychotherapy, see the clinical services overview.

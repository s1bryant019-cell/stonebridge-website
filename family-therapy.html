Family Therapy

Change the pattern the family is carrying.

Stonebridge offers relational family therapy for Illinois families who want to understand recurring patterns, communicate more clearly, and respond to stress without placing the entire problem on one person.

When a concern belongs to the whole system

A family can become stuck even when everyone is trying.

A concern may appear centered on one person while affecting relationships throughout the family. Therapy looks at the interaction patterns that keep everyone caught, with room for different perspectives and responsibility without blame.

Reasons families seek therapy

Different concerns can place pressure on the same relationships.

Conflict & communication

Address recurring escalation, withdrawal, or misunderstanding that makes it difficult to express needs and hear one another.

Parent–adolescent development

Navigate changing independence, privacy, identity, school demands, and responsibilities between parents and teens.

Transitions & changing roles

Work through shifts related to separation, remarriage, caregiving, relocation, illness, or grief.

Mental health or substance-use impact

Address how mental health or substance-use concerns affect family relationships when outpatient work is appropriate.

Trust & repair

Acknowledge hurt and rebuild predictability after secrecy, disappointment, or repeated ruptures.

How the work is approached

Each person matters, and so does what happens between them.

01 · System

Understand the system.

Identify recurring interactions while making room for each person’s experience and responsibility.

02 · Boundaries

Clarify roles and boundaries.

Distinguish support from control, avoidance, or blurred roles, with attention to development, privacy, safety, and responsibility.

03 · Practice

Practice different responses.

Use clearer communication, predictable limits, and repair when familiar patterns return outside therapy.

A flexible but defined treatment frame

Family therapy does not require every family member to attend every session. Participation depends on treatment goals, clinical appropriateness, and each person’s role in the concern. Expectations around privacy, individual contact, records, communication, and decision-making are discussed before ongoing work begins.

Clinical fit and safety

Family therapy is not emergency care, legal advice, custody evaluation, parenting coordination, reunification therapy, or a court-directed opinion. Safety, coercion, and the appropriateness of conjoint treatment are reviewed throughout care.

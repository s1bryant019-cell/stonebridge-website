# Stonebridge Image Art Direction

Use this rule for all future page imagery. Images should reinforce Stonebridge's telehealth model, established brand character, and restrained visual system rather than introduce a generic psychotherapy aesthetic.

Every new image should belong to one of three families:

1. **Real Stonebridge** — Dr. Bryant, future clinicians, and genuine practice-specific material.
2. **Telehealth / home context** — quiet, natural environments where clients actually receive care, including home or workspace settings that feel lived-in rather than staged.
3. **Stonebridge environmental language** — bridges, paths, thresholds, architecture, light, transition, and related imagery that supports the brand without becoming literal or decorative for its own sake.

Avoid generic therapist-and-client stock scenes, empty luxury therapy offices, imagery that implies clients physically visit Stonebridge, random abstract wellness imagery, stacked stones, beaches, lotus imagery, or lifestyle clichés such as hands holding coffee.

Maintain warm natural light, restrained neutrals, wood tones, and muted blue accents. Do not introduce an image simply to make a page more visually busy. The image should clarify the service, reinforce the brand, or provide useful atmosphere; otherwise, omit it.
